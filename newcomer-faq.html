Moving to northern Utah

Newcomer FAQ

Inversions, snow, taxes, water shares and the other things people ask before moving to northern Utah.

Weather & air

What are the seasons like?

Four distinct seasons. In Logan, January averages a high of 32°F and a low of 18°F, and July averages 88°F and 60°F, with very little summer rain. Ogden runs a few degrees warmer. Ogden Valley and Bear Lake are colder and snowier. Fall color peaks in late September to early October. Every town guide has an "A year in…" section with local averages.

Source: NOAA climate normals

What is a winter inversion?

On calm winter days, high pressure can trap cold air, and the pollution in it, under warmer air in a bowl-shaped valley like Cache Valley. Fine-particle pollution can then build to unhealthy levels until a storm clears it out. Researchers at Utah State University estimate that ammonium nitrate, formed when ammonia mixes with vehicle exhaust, makes up about half or more of northern Utah's winter pollution. Inversions come and go with the weather, mostly December through February.

Source: USU: Toward fewer bad air days

What are no-burn days, and how do I check the air?

The Utah Division of Air Quality usually calls mandatory no-burn days between Nov. 1 and Mar. 1 in parts of Cache, Box Elder, Weber and other counties. On those days wood, pellet and coal stoves and fireplaces can't be used; natural gas and propane are fine. Check air.utah.gov, sign up for its email alerts or use the free UtahAir app.

Source: Utah DEQ: no-burn days

How much snow falls?

It depends a lot on where you live. Based on NOAA's 1991–2020 averages, Brigham City gets about 38 inches a year, Logan about 62, Laketown at Bear Lake about 84 and Pineview Dam in Ogden Valley about 108. Most snow falls November through April.

Source: NOAA climate normals

What should I know about winter driving?

UDOT can require snow-rated tires or chains on canyon highways during storms, including Logan Canyon (US-89), Sardine Canyon (US-89/91), the road to Powder Mountain (SR-158) and the road to Snowbasin (SR-226). Logan Canyon between Logan and Garden City can close for avalanche control. Check the UDOT Traffic app before you go.

Source: UDOT traction law · UDOT Traffic app

Money & taxes

How does the cost of living compare?

The federal Bureau of Economic Analysis puts overall prices in the Logan metro area about 4% below the U.S. average (2024), and Utah as a whole about 1% below. Housing costs vary more than that by town. Coming from Southern California? See the side-by-side cost comparison.

Source: BEA via FRED: Logan metro · Utah

What is Utah's income tax rate?

Utah has a flat individual income tax of 4.45% for 2026, after the Legislature trimmed it from 4.5%. Ask a tax professional about your own situation.

Source: Utah SB 60 (2026)

What is the sales tax, and are groceries taxed?

Groceries (unprepared food) are taxed at a reduced 3% statewide. The combined general rate is 7.30% in Logan, about 6.95% in Brigham City and about 7.45% in Ogden; Weber County added 0.20% on Oct. 1, 2026, so check the Tax Commission's current table for a specific address.

Source: Utah food tax rate · Utah sales tax rates

How do property taxes work?

A primary residence gets a 45% exemption, so property tax is figured on 55% of the home's market value (the exemption covers the home and up to one acre). Second homes and vacant residential property are taxed on 100% of value. You may need to file with the county assessor to get the exemption. The Tax Foundation puts Utah's effective rate on owner-occupied homes at about 0.48%.

Source: Utah Tax Commission · Tax Foundation

Buying a home here

What are water shares and secondary water?

Many homes have secondary (pressurized irrigation) water for the yard, separate from drinking water, and Utah requires every secondary connection to be metered by Jan. 1, 2030. Water rights are not automatically attached to a property. Ask whether the home has secondary water and from whom, whether any water rights or irrigation company shares come with the sale, and how they will be transferred.

Source: Utah Division of Water Rights · SB 251 (2023)

Should I test for radon?

Yes. Utah DEQ reports that about 1 in 3 Utah homes tested were above the EPA action level. Testing is the only way to know; the state sells low-cost kits at radon.utah.gov, and a mitigation system typically runs about $1,500.

Source: Utah DEQ: radon

What about earthquakes?

The Wasatch Front, including the Ogden area, has real earthquake risk: a state working group estimated a 43% chance of a magnitude 6.75+ quake somewhere on the Wasatch Front within 50 years. Standard homeowners policies generally don't cover earthquake damage; it's added separately.

Source: KSL: Utah earthquake probabilities

Is wildfire a concern on the benches?

Homes next to open wildland can be. Since Jan. 1, 2026, Utah maps a "high-risk wildland-urban interface," and taxable structures inside it pay a small annual fee ($20–$100 per structure in 2026–27). Look up any address on the Utah Wildfire Risk Assessment Portal.

Source: Utah HB 48 summary · Wildfire risk portal

Settling in

How do I register my car? Do I need an emissions test?

New residents have 60 days to title and register vehicles in Utah, and a car titled here for the first time needs a VIN inspection. Most passenger cars no longer need a safety inspection. Cache County requires emissions tests every other year for gasoline vehicles 6+ years old; Weber County tests newer vehicles every other year and older ones yearly. Box Elder County has no emissions testing.

Source: Utah DMV · Cache County motor vehicle

When do I need a Utah driver license?

Once you establish residency. Bring your old license and identity, Social Security and residency documents; expect a vision test and possibly a knowledge test. Confirm current requirements with the Utah Driver License Division before your appointment.

Source: Utah Driver License Division

How do I set up utilities?

It depends on the city. Logan runs its own power, water, sewer and garbage service (start at Logan utility billing, 290 N 100 W). Rocky Mountain Power serves Ogden and many other cities, and Enbridge Gas Utah (formerly Dominion Energy) provides natural gas statewide. Each town guide lists its providers.

Source: Logan utility billing

Where are the hospitals?

Ogden has two Level II trauma centers, McKay-Dee Hospital and Ogden Regional Medical Center. Logan Regional Hospital is a Level III trauma center, and Brigham City Community Hospital is Level IV.

Source: Utah Bureau of EMS trauma centers

How far is the airport?

Salt Lake City International is about 89 miles (roughly 1½ hours) from Logan and much closer from Ogden, and Salt Lake Express runs a reservation shuttle from Logan. Ogden-Hinckley Airport has limited service; as of early 2026 Breeze Airways flew to Orange County (John Wayne Airport).

Source: USU: maps & transportation · Breeze in Ogden

Is there public transit?

Cache Valley's buses, now called Connect Public Transit, have been fare-free since 1992 and serve Logan and nearby towns. In Weber County, UTA's FrontRunner commuter rail runs from Ogden and Roy to Salt Lake City and Provo, Monday through Saturday.

Source: Connect Public Transit · UTA FrontRunner
